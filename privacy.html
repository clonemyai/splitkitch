Privacy Policy

Privacy, stated plainly

SplitKitch is neighbors sharing food. Running it takes a small amount of information, held carefully, and this page is the whole list.

Last updated November 16, 2026. This covers the splitkitch.com website, the SplitKitch web app, and the SplitKitch apps for iPhone and Android.

The short version

An account is one phone number and a code. Everything else on your profile is yours to add, or not. Your exact address is never published, your phone number is never shown to other users, and we never sell personal data. There are no advertising trackers, and the iOS app ships with no analytics SDK at all. Food money moves directly between buyers and sellers, so we never see it, hold it, or record it.

One phone number, on purpose

A profile takes one phone number and nothing else. We use it for exactly one thing: sending the one-time code that signs you in. It lives only in your private account record. It never appears on your profile, your listings, or anywhere another user can see it.

And stated plainly, the same way we state it in the app: we confirm the number is real and reachable, and that is all it means. It is not an identity check, and a phone number does not make anyone verified.

What you add is your choice

Everything past the phone number is voluntary, added by you, in any order, or not at all. New accounts start with no name at all. If you add them, we store:

  • A display name, a rough neighborhood label, and a short bio. The neighborhood is an area name, never an address.
  • A profile photo, if you set one.
  • Permit details, if you post paid food: the permit type, number, and issuing county. Paid listings require them. Wherever they appear, on your profile and your listings, they carry the standing label: self-reported by the seller, not verified by the platform, listed for convenience only, with a link so anyone can check the permit with the county directly.
  • A kitchen application, if you run a food bank, shelter, or community kitchen: your organization's name and its EIN or food-bank network affiliation. A person reviews these by hand.

Your public profile shows your display name, neighborhood, photo, your ratings in each role, and your standing: Kitch points and your lifetime 5-star count, which only ever go up. It never shows your phone number.

Listings, claims, and addresses

Listings are public while they are live. A listing carries a neighborhood label and a position that is jittered before it is stored, shifted by up to about half a mile, so a listing can never be triangulated to a house. An exact home address is never stored on a listing.

The exact pickup address exists in exactly one place: the claim, the private record between one buyer and one seller. The seller writes it when they confirm the claim, and only those two people can ever read it. The claim also carries a 4-digit pickup code, spoken or shown at the door.

One honest exception: food bank and community kitchen distributions carry the organization's public distribution address on purpose. Those are public events, and the address is the point.

Listings are built to disappear. They leave the feed when the food is claimed or the window closes, stay readable for 24 hours after they end, and then are no longer public. After a completed handoff, both sides can leave a rating; ratings are public, because they are the trust signal everyone else relies on.

Photos

Photos you upload, for a listing or your profile, are shrunk and re-encoded on your own device or browser before they are sent. Re-encoding rebuilds the image from its pixels, which strips EXIF and GPS data by construction. Your originals are never uploaded. Processed photos are stored with the services below.

Location

The apps never ask for, and never read, your device's GPS location. You choose your area; listings are placed by neighborhood, with the jitter described above. If you visit from outside the areas we serve, the app may record one coarse demand count: a grid cell about seven miles wide and a rough area label, so we know where to open next. Nothing precise, and nothing that identifies you.

Eat-time pings

Notifications are opt-in. If you turn on eat-time pings, we store a push token for your device, your area, your timezone, and up to three time windows you choose. A fresh listing posted inside one of your windows sends you a ping; outside them, silence. You can turn pings off at any time.

Who processes it

SplitKitch runs on Google's Firebase services, which process the data above on our behalf: Firebase Authentication (sign-in codes), Cloud Firestore (the database), Firebase Storage (photos), and Firebase Cloud Messaging (eat-time pings). We do not sell personal data, share it with data brokers, or use it for advertising. This website, splitkitch.com, sets no cookies and runs no analytics or trackers.

Keeping it, and deleting it

Your account information is kept while your account exists. You can delete your account in the app, from your Profile. Here is what deletion removes:

  • Your listings, pulled from the feed
  • The pickup details stored on your claims
  • Your private account record, including your phone number
  • Your public profile, including your points and board standing
  • Your kitchen application, if you made one
  • Your profile photo
  • Your eat-time ping subscription
  • Your sign-in account itself

If you would rather write to us first, use the contact below and we will help.

Things we will never do

  • Sell your personal data, or hand it to data brokers.
  • Show your phone number to other users.
  • Publish your home address on a listing.
  • Read your device's location. The apps never ask for it.
  • Track you for advertising. No advertising trackers, anywhere.
